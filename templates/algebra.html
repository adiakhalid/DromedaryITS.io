    Algebra

    Algebra deals with variables (do not have fixed values) and arithmetic operations. In algebraic expressions, these values are usually represented using symbols like \(x, y, z\), etc. These symbols are manipulated using various arithmetic operations (such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division) with the goal to find their values. An algebraic equation can be represented by \(2x + 3 = 16\). In this expression, 3 and 16 are constants that remain fixed; additionally, 3 is a coefficient, whereas '\(x\)' is the variable.

    Algebraic expression

    An algebraic expression or polynomial is an expression made up of variables, constants, arithmetic expressions and positive integers exponents of variables. Two algebraic expressions joined by an 'equal to (=)' symbol form an equation, which is a mathematical statement. The different types of equations, based on the degree of the variables are given below:

  • Linear Equations

    Linear equations are equations with degree one. Their standard form is \(ax + b = 0\), where a and b are constants and \(x\) is a variable.
    In the example given below, we will solve a linear algebraic expression with basic steps:
    Example: \(5x - 15 = 2x - 6\)
    Step1: Transfer \(2x\) to left-hand-side (L.H.S) of equation and change the sign from positive to negative. Also transfer -15 to right-hand-side (R.H.S) and change negative sign to positive.
    \(\implies 5x - 2x = -6 + 15\)
    Step 2: Solve basic arithmetic expressions.
    \(\implies 3x = 9\)
    Divide both sides by a common divisor such as 3
    \(\implies 3x / 3 = 9 / 3\)
    \(\implies x = 3\)

  • Quadratic Equations

    A quadratic equation is an equation of degree two and can be written in the standard form as \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), where x is the variable, the term \(ax^2\) has degree 2, the term \(bx\) has degree 1, \(c\) is the constant term (degree 0), and \(a, b, c\) are constants.
    Example: \(4x^2 - 15x + 9 = 0\)
    Step 1: Split the middle term '\(-15x\)' into two terms whose sum equals \(b\) and whose product equals \(a \cdot c\) (here, \(a \cdot c = 4 \cdot 9 = 36\)).
    \(\implies 4x^2 - 12x - 3x + 9=0 \)
    \(\implies 4x (x - 3) - 3 (x - 3)=0\)
    \(\implies (4x - 3)(x - 3) = 0\)
    Step 2: Therefore, one of \((4x - 3)\) and \((x - 3)\) must be zero,
    when, \(4x - 3 = 0 \implies x = 3 / 4 \)
    and when \(x - 3 = 0 \implies x = 3 \)
    Thus required solutions are \(x = 3 / 4, 3\).
