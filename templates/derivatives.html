Derivatives

The derivative is the instantaneous rate of change at which output of a function changes as its input value changes. The rate of change of a function \(f(x)\), often denoted as \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(f(x)\right)\), \(\dfrac{df}{dx}\), \({D}{f}\left(x\right)\), or \({f'}\left(x\right) = 0\). Derivative is used to optimize a function also it find the intervals where the function is increasing or decreasing.
Given below are the basic rules of derivatives:

Constant Rule

According to the derivatives constant rule, the derivatives of a constant is always be 0. If \(y = c\), where \(c\) is a constant, then \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{dc}{dx} = 0\).
Example: \(y = 20\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(20\right) = 0\).

Constant Multiplication Rule

It states that if you have a function that is a constant multiplied by another function, the derivative of this expression is the constant multiplied by the derivative of the other function. If \(y = cx\), where \(c\) is a constant, then \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\left(cx\right) = c \cdot \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x\right)\).
Example: \(y = 7x\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(7x\right)\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 7 \cdot \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x\right)\)     (i.e., \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x\right) = 1\))
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 7\)

Power Rule

According to the power rule, if we have \(y = x^n\), then \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = n \cdot x^{n-1}\).
Example: \(y = x^3\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x^3\right)\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3 \cdot x^{3-1}\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2\)
Example: \(y = x^5\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 5x^{5-1} = 5x^4\)

Sum/Difference Rule

According to the sum and difference rule, the derivative distributes over addition and subtraction as: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}[a \pm b] = \dfrac{da}{dx} \pm \dfrac{db}{dx}\).
Example: \(y = 2x \pm 7\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(2x \pm 7\right)\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(2x\right) \pm \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(7\right)\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2 \pm 0\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2\)

Product Rule

The rule for finding the derivative of a product of two functions states that: “the derivative of the first function multiplied by the second function, plus the first function multiplied by the derivative of the second function.” Such as \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(a \cdot b\right) = a \cdot \dfrac{db}{dx} + b \cdot \dfrac{da}{dx}\)   ("\(\cdot\)" is alternatively used for multiplication).
Example: \(y = 3x^3 \times 5x\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(3x^3 \times 5x\right)\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^3 \cdot \dfrac{d}{dx}(5x) + 5x \cdot \dfrac{d}{dx}(3x^3)\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^3 \cdot 5 + 5x \cdot 9x^2\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 15x^3 + 45x^3\)
\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 60x^3\)

Trigonometric Functions

These derivative formulas are essential for finding the rates of change of trigonometric functions and their inverses in calculus and physics problems.
\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\sin(x)\right) = \cos(x)\)
\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\cos(x)\right) = -\sin(x)\)
\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\tan(x)\right) = \sec^2(x)\)
\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\sin^{-1}(x)\right) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}\)
\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\cos^{-1}(x)\right) = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}\)
\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\tan^{-1}(x)\right) = \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}\)
